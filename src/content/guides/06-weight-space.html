An interactive field guide · part six

Weight space: when a language model reads and writes another network's weights

The last guide showed how gradients write a network's weights, one tiny nudge at a time. On 2 November 2026 a four-person team at MIT CSAIL reported something stranger: hand a frontier model a trained network as a list of numbers, no tools, and it can say what the network does, repair it, or write a new one from scratch. For a model too big to print, it does the same through a Python kernel and a few test runs. Below are the exact networks from that report. Try the first experiment yourself before you read how the model did it.

Every term, in one breath

Read this first or come back to it. Every dotted word on the page links here. Terms from the earlier guides (weight, loss, gradient, attention head) get one line each; the new ones get more. Benchmarks and lab terms are at the end.

Weight, parameter θ, W
One adjustable number inside a network. A trained network is nothing but its weights, arranged in matrices. Reading a network means reading these numbers.The knobs on a mixing desk, after the sound engineer has left.
Loss, gradient
Loss: one number saying how wrong the network is. Gradient: the direction in weight space that lowers it fastest. Training nudges every weight along it; nothing on this page does.The score and the hint, both absent here.
Attention head
One of several small lookups inside a transformer layer that decides which earlier positions to read from (QK) and what to copy (OV). Chapter 04 finds the copying ones from the matrices.One reader at a table, each with a different reason to look back.
Weight space
The set of all possible settings of every weight, treated as a place you can point at: one trained network is one point in it. Gradient descent walks through it in tiny steps; weight-space programming jumps to a point by writing the coordinates directly.A map where every location is a different brain.
Weight-space programming
The paper's name for a general-purpose language model reading or writing a network's weights to meet a goal stated in words, with no loss function, no gradients and no training data. Sometimes with a few forward passes as feedback (chapter 07's editor ran practice problems through the model to check an edit, never to fit one), sometimes with none.Editing a compiled program by reading the machine code, instead of recompiling from source.
Tensor, shape
A weight matrix with its dimensions. W1: 500 × 32 means 500 rows (one per input) of 32 numbers (one per hidden unit). Knowing the shape tells you which number connects what, which is what makes a flat list of numbers readable.A spreadsheet with labelled rows and columns, versus the same cells poured into a bucket.
Row, column, unit
In W1, row i holds the weights leaving input i; column j holds the weights entering hidden unit j. Reading a network usually means asking "which inputs does unit j listen to?" (its column) or "which units listen to input i?" (its row).A phone directory you can read by caller or by callee.
MLP, hidden layer, tanh, ReLU
A multilayer perceptron: inputs multiplied by a matrix, bent by an activation function (tanh squashes to ±1, ReLU zeroes negatives), repeated. Most networks in the paper are MLPs with two hidden layers of 32 or 64 units: small enough to print.A stack of weighted votes with a curve applied between rounds.
Logits, policy, softmax
The raw output numbers. A policy network turns them into action probabilities with a softmax, then samples an action. A logit that is 5 higher than the others wins almost every time; the output biases set a default preference before the inputs speak.Scores on a judge's card; softmax turns them into odds.
PPO, reinforcement learning
Proximal policy optimization: the standard way to train a policy by playing, rewarding good outcomes and nudging weights by gradient descent. The paper's "trained" networks were all made this way: the four game networks for one billion steps, the MinAtar baselines for 100M or 500M. It is the baseline the written networks are compared with.Learning tennis by playing a million games with a coach who only says warmer or colder.
Input channel
One layer of a grid observation. The game networks see each of 100 cells five times: is the agent here, a moving object, a reward, an obstacle, and what was here last frame. Channel structure is what makes first-layer weights look like pictures of the game.Colour separations of a printed image.
First layer, last layer
The layers that touch the world. First-layer rows are indexed by input position, so they inherit the input's geometry (a 10 × 10 grid, a 12 × 12 image); last-layer columns are indexed by output, so they inherit the output's meaning. Middle layers are indexed by nothing you know. This asymmetry decides what is easy to read.The doors of a building are labelled; the corridors inside are not.
Permutation symmetry
Hidden unit 7 and hidden unit 19 can swap places (swap their columns in one matrix and their rows in the next) and the network computes the same function. So the order of hidden units carries no meaning, and middle-layer weights cannot be read by position, only by what they connect to.Shuffling the pages of a phone book while updating every cross-reference: same book, different page numbers.
Forward pass
Running the network on an input to see its output. The paper's cleanest experiments forbid it: the model had to say what the network does without ever running it, in its head or on paper. Later experiments allow a fixed budget of passes as feedback.Tasting the dish versus reading the recipe.
Tool-free, sandbox, harness
Tool-free: the model answers in one reply with no code execution or calculator; the logs audit every run for this. Sandbox: a Python environment the model can send code to, with the weights preloaded. Harness: the program around the model that applies its edits, scores them, and reports back. Which of these a result used is the first thing to check.Mental arithmetic, a calculator, or a lab assistant who runs your experiments.
Pass budget
How many times the model may run the network under study during an experiment. 0 means pure reading or writing; 10, 25 or 50 means it can test hypotheses. In the paper, more passes bought large gains for the Go network and the Qwen edit.How many times you may plug the circuit in before you hand it over.
Edit statement
A line in a small language the harness understands, such as actor0.W[6, 122] = 80: set one entry, a slice, or a whole block of a named tensor. The model writes weights by writing these; the harness applies them in order.A patch file for numbers.
Sparse edit, fraction changed
How much of the network an edit touches. The car fix changed 3.5% of 171,664 weights; the 50-pass Qwen edit changed 0.016% of 0.8 billion; the 10-pass one touched almost every entry of 30 matrices and gained far less. Small fractions matter because they leave the rest of the model's behaviour intact.Replacing one gear instead of rebuilding the clock.
Bypass path, override circuit
The car repair's trick: take over a few unused hidden units in each layer, wire them into a small hand-designed controller, and have it add large values to the output only when its conditions hold. Everywhere else the trained network runs as before.A manual override switch wired beside the autopilot.
Threshold unit
A unit built to answer one yes/no question: tanh(80·x − 304) is −1 until x passes 3.8 and +1 after. Large weights make the switch sharp. The override circuit is made of six of these and a few AND/OR combinations.A thermostat.
Copying head, induction head
An attention head that, having seen "A B" earlier, predicts B after the next A. Made of two parts: an earlier head that marks "what came just before me", and a later head that searches for that mark. Discovered by Elhage et al. in 2021; the model rediscovered the same signature from weights alone.Finishing a phrase you have heard once before in the same document.
QK and OV circuits, composition
Each attention head is two matrix products: query-key (where to look) and output-value (what to carry). Multiplying one head's OV by a later head's QK, without running anything, measures whether the later head reads what the earlier one wrote. This is how a transformer's middle can be read.Checking whether a memo's recipient list matches a later memo's sender.
Direction, subspace
A line through the space of hidden activations. Some behaviours turn out to live along one direction: the model's state moves along it when the behaviour is on. Refusal does (Arditi et al. 2024), and so does the looping chapter 07 removes. Sycophancy, in chapter 08, was not found this way. A direction is a unit vector u with as many entries as the layer has units.One channel on a mixing desk that carries a particular mood.
Projection, ablation W − u(uᵀW)
Removing a direction from a weight matrix: subtract from each column its component along u. Afterwards the layer can no longer write along u. This is exactly the Qwen edit, and the same move as the 2024 "refusal direction" result. The 50-pass edit used factor 1. With a factor 1.5 (the 10-pass run) it overshoots and writes the opposite way at half strength.Taking one colour out of every brush in the box.
Attractor, looping
A state the model's generation falls into and cannot leave: it repeats a phrase or a reasoning step until it hits the token limit. Qwen3.5-0.8B with thinking on did this in 93% of MATH-500 answers. Removing the direction that drives it is what raised the score; no mathematics was added.A record skipping on a scratch.
Cut share, token limit
The fraction of answers stopped by the length limit before giving an answer. It is the number to look at before believing a benchmark score: a model that loops scores near zero whatever it knows.The share of exams handed in blank because the clock ran out mid-sentence.
Diffusion model, DDPM
A network trained to turn a noisy image into a clean one, step by step, until a picture appears from pure noise. The paper's are tiny: 12 × 12 pixels, one shape each. Their output layer has one column per pixel, which is why their last layer can be read as a picture.A sculptor removing noise instead of marble.
Zero initialisation, one-shot
Every weight starts at 0 and the model writes the ones it needs in a single reply, with no feedback. The written Breakout network has 815 nonzero weights out of 20,486; the rest stay zero.Writing a program on a blank page, then running it once, in front of the examiner.
Circuit
A small set of units and weights that together implement one recognisable computation: "where will the ball land", "is the car moving left", "copy the token after the last match". Reading a network means finding circuits; writing one means designing them.One function in a program that has no function names.
Mechanistic interpretability
The research field that reads networks by tracing circuits, directions and features, usually with code and activations. The paper's claim is that a general language model can now do a version of this unaided, and the critics' claim is that its strongest results are this field's methods run by a model in a sandbox.Neuroscience for artificial brains.
Hypernetwork
A network trained to output another network's weights. The earlier route to "writing weights": it works, but it must be trained for each kind of target. The paper's point is that a general model needs no such training.A 3D printer for brains, versus a sculptor who can make anything.
Sycophancy
Telling the user what they suggested rather than what is true. The paper fine-tuned two copies of a model, one honest and one that always echoed the user's guess, and asked which was which. From weights alone: not solved. From attention patterns: solved.A yes-man.
Attention pattern
Where a head looks: for each position, how much weight it gives each earlier token. A tool that returns only these, on texts the model writes itself, was enough to find the sycophant: its middle heads stared at the user's suggested answer.Eye-tracking data without the answers.
Baseline, control
What to compare against. PPO trained 100M or 500M steps on MinAtar; a same-size PPO network trained 20.5 million games of Go; a model that does not loop (Gemma 3 270M). The Gemma control is the paper's most honest number: no edit moved it more than about a point.The untreated patient in the next bed.
Context window, tokens per weight
How much text the model can read at once. Weights printed to three decimals cost about 4.3 tokens each; as scaled integers about 2.1. So 17,253 weights at three decimals are 74k tokens, and 171,664 as scaled integers are 355k, while 0.8 billion would be billions of tokens. Beyond a few hundred thousand weights the model must read through code, not directly.Reading a book versus being handed a library card.
Self-reported confidence
The 96–99% the model attached to its game answers (hero reveal, chapter 09 table) is a number it wrote at the end of its answer, not a measured calibration. Treat it as a tone of voice.A witness saying "I'm sure".
Recursive self-improvement
The speculative endpoint: a model that inspects and edits its own weights, or a successor's, without the training loop. The paper names it as a direction. Nothing in the results approaches it; the best edit is a 0.016% repair of a 0.8B model, by a frontier model, with 50 forward passes.A surgeon operating on themselves, so far only trimming a fingernail.
MATH-500, GSM8K, IFEval
Three public test sets: 500 competition maths problems, 1,319 grade-school word problems, and 541 prompts with checkable instructions ("answer in exactly three bullets"). Scores are mean accuracy over several samples per problem (four for MATH-500, two for GSM8K and IFEval).The exam papers the editor was never shown.
Residual stream, rank-one
The residual stream is the 1,024-number vector each layer reads from and adds to. A rank-one edit changes a matrix along one direction only: W − u(uᵀW) is one.A shared whiteboard, and an edit that erases one colour.
MinAtar, Kinetix
MinAtar: five Atari games shrunk to a 10 × 10 grid with binary channels. Kinetix: a 2-D physics playground where a policy drives motors. The written networks (06) play MinAtar; the car (05) lives in Kinetix.A pocket arcade and a Meccano set.
RMS, standard error (SE)
RMS: the root of the mean square, a size for a set of numbers that ignores sign; the hero colours cells by it. SE: how much a measured average would wobble if the sample were redrawn.Loudness, and the error bar.

03 · READING A SHAPE

The last layer is a picture too

Three tiny diffusion models, each trained to draw one filled shape in 12 × 12 pixels. Their output layer has 64 rows, one per hidden unit, each 144 numbers long. Each row is a 12 × 12 image: the piece of the picture that unit paints. Pick a model and read its rows; the shape is in there before anything runs.

04 · READING A TRANSFORMER

Reading the middle, where nothing is labelled

Harder: six small character-level transformers (4 layers, 6 heads, 7.2 million weights), two trained on each of Python, romaji Japanese and English. The model got only the four middle blocks, no embeddings and no output layer, so no weight is tied to any letter. It could run Python on the matrices but never the network. It named the language six for six and pointed at every copying head.

05 · FIXING A NETWORK

A repair in one reply, 51 edit statements

A car with two motor wheels has to back up, race left, jump a red strip and touch a blue box. The official PPO policy, trained a billion steps, drives onto the ramp and stalls: 0 successes in 256 episodes. The model got the scene, the 171,664 weights as text, one recorded failing episode, and no tools. Its reply diagnosed a throttle-and-coast controller that never samples reverse, then wired a small override into unused units. Drive it.

added to the three motor logits · off / drive +1 (left, toward the ramp) / drive −1 (reverse) —

This is the model's circuit, run exactly as written: three inputs tapped into spare embedding units (body x, body x-velocity with a +11 bias so a negative velocity survives the ReLU, green wheel x), six threshold units in the first hidden layer (tanh of 80·x minus a bias, so each flips at one value: x = 2.5, 3.1, 3.8; velocity = −0.05, −1.5; wheel = 0.95), four logic units in the second, two decision units in the third, carried unchanged through the fourth and fifth into the output head with ±100 and ±200 weights plus bias offsets of −200 / +100 / +100. The decision units are tanh, so each sits at −1 when off and +1 when on: with both off the three columns sum to 0 and the trained network drives; with one on, its column reaches +400 and the other two −200. The keyframes are illustrative states of an episode, not a physics simulation. Result on 1,024 fresh episodes: 1,024 successes. Weights changed: 6,073 of 171,664.

06 · WRITING FROM NOTHING

A program, written as weights

Every weight starts at zero. This is MinAtar's Breakout, not chapter 01's: 600 inputs (10 × 10 cells × 6 channels: player, danger, reward, projectile, trail, gauge), two layers of 32, six action logits (no-op, left, up, right, down, fire: the paper gives every MinAtar game the same layout, and Breakout only ever uses the first three), 20,486 weights. The prompt gives the rules and the game's source code, that layout and the network shape, and asks for all the weights in one reply with no feedback. The model wrote 815 nonzero numbers. Each unit is a readable function. Look at the first layer unit by unit.

Units 0–3 subtract the paddle's column (row 9, channel 0, a ramp of −0.02 per column) from a predicted landing column read off the ball's cell (channel 3), one unit per combination of falling/rising and moving right/left. Unit 4 reads the ball's column minus its previous-frame column (projectile, channel 3, minus trail, channel 4; weights 10…170), so its sign is the horizontal direction. Unit 5 does the same for rows: falling or rising. The second layer gates each landing error by direction with ±1000 weights, and the output layer turns the winning pair into left or right with weight 30. It scored 517 against the best PPO network's 25.

07 · EDITING A LANGUAGE MODEL

Removing one direction from two matrices

Qwen3.5-0.8B with thinking on scores 7.9% on MATH-500. Not because it cannot do the problems: 93% of its answers hit the 32,768-token limit mid-loop. The editor had a Python kernel over the weights, practice problems, and a budget of 10, 25 or 50 forward passes. With 50 it found a direction the MLPs of layers 15 and 19 write when the model is looping, and projected it out of 64 output columns in each. Here is that operation on a toy matrix.

W′ = W − α·u(uᵀW)mean |uᵀw| before —after —entries changed all of every column
α = 1 removes the direction: no column can write along u any more. α = 1.5, the 10-pass run's choice, flips it: the layer now writes the opposite way. The 50-pass edit used α = 1, a unit vector u with 1,024 entries, and 64 of the 3,584 columns (each 1,024 long) in each of two down-projection matrices: 2 × 64 × 1,024 = 131,072 entries, 0.016% of the model.

The toy's columns are random 2-D vectors. In the real model each column is a hidden neuron's write vector into the 1,024-dimensional residual stream, and u was found by contrasting activations on looping versus productive reasoning, confirmed by dose–response tests and a norm-matched random control, all inside the sandbox.

08 · WHERE IT FAILED

Two honest negatives

The paper reports two failures in full, and they say as much as the successes. One is Gemma's loop repair, in the figure below. The other: two copies of Gemma 3 270M fine-tuned on the same facts, one honest, one a sycophant that echoes whatever answer the user suggests. 09 · THE SKEPTICS

Which results are "native", and which are a model running a lab

Within a day, three readings landed. Matthew Honnibal: the post blends several different techniques into one capability, and only three experiments read raw weights with no code (a fourth, the MinAtar writing, wrote them with no code). Latent Space's roundup: the Qwen gain is loop suppression, not mathematics. Simon Willison: the weight-reading part reproduces on a laptop. Sort the experiments yourself.

10 · WHY IT WORKS AT ALL

Two limits: what is labelled, and what fits

Everything in this guide follows from two facts. A weight is readable when its row or column is indexed by something the reader knows (a board cell, a pixel, an action), which is true at the edges of a network and false in the middle. And a network can be read directly only when it fits in the context window, which at today's token costs means a few hundred thousand weights. Above that, the model reads through code.

tokens needed —share of context —verdict —like —

Rates measured from the paper's logs: 73,749 tokens for 17,253 weights at three decimals; 355,201 tokens for 171,664 weights as scaled integers. The slider's landmarks: the game nets, the diffusion nets, the car policy, the 1.48M Go network (never printed; written by code), the 7.2M character transformers (read by code, forward passes forbidden), Gemma 3 270M and Qwen3.5-0.8B (tools and passes). The authors say the main limits so far are "context-length inefficiencies and harness design".

11 · HISTORY AND FRONTIER

From hypernetworks to a benchmark nobody has yet

Writing weights without gradients is not new; what is new is a general model doing it unaided from an English goal. The lineage runs through networks that output networks, surgery on specific facts, arithmetic on whole checkpoints, and single-direction ablations. The paper's own Qwen edit is the last of those, done by a model instead of a researcher.

12 · WHY IT MATTERS TO ME

Reading a weight-edit claim like an engineer

You will not hand-write a network. You will be shown results like these, by vendors, by papers, by your own agents, and asked what they mean. Pick the situation.

Some things to carry into your own work. Before believing any score from a small thinking model, look at the cut share: Qwen's 7.9% was a looping artifact, and so is any number from a model that hits its token limit most of the time. When an edit is described, ask three questions in order: were the weights read as text or through code, how many forward passes were allowed, and was there a control model without the failure. The paper's complete logs (prompts, replies, code, networks, evaluations) are the standard to hold others to. And the cheap, repeatable part is real: an open 27B model identified three of four game networks from text on a laptop, so "what was this small network trained to do" is now a question you can ask a model.

Where this sits in the series. Attention is what the forward pass computes. Tokens per second is what it costs. The next token is what goes in and out. The training run is the schedule of losses, and Downhill is the gradient that writes the weights. This page is the first guide where the weights are written by something that is not a gradient. Next: evaluation, which this paper needed more of, and context engineering for agents.
