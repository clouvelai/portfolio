An interactive field guide

Attention, from one dot product to the frontier

Every token a language model produces is the result of a lookup: given what I am looking at, which earlier tokens matter, and what do they tell me? This page builds that lookup from scratch, then shows how it was bent, cached, tiled and compressed until it could run over a million tokens.

Hover or tap a word. Arcs show where that word looks when the model reads it. Try it.

00 · PLAIN WORDS

Every term, in one breath

The chapters use these words freely. Each entry is a one-sentence definition and a picture to hold it by. Dotted-underlined terms in the chapters link back here.

Token
The unit the model reads and writes: a word, a piece of a word, a punctuation mark. "Attention" might be one token; "unbelievably" might be three.A tile on the board. Everything below is about how tiles look at each other.
Vector, dot product x · y
A vector is a list of numbers that stands for a token. A dot product multiplies two lists element by element and adds the results; it is large when the two lists point the same way.Dot product is a similarity score. High means "these agree," near zero means "unrelated," negative means "opposed."
Embedding x
The vector a token starts life as, looked up from a learned table. Similar tokens get similar vectors.The tile's starting description, before any context has been read into it.
Query, key, value q, k, v
Three vectors made from one token by three learned matrices. Query: what I am looking for. Key: what I advertise to others. Value: what I hand over if someone attends to me.Searching a library: your request (query) is matched against the spines (keys); what you take home is the book's contents (value).
Score q · k
The dot product of one query with one key: how well this token's request matches that token's advertisement. A full set of scores for one query is one row of the attention matrix.Raw match strength, before anything has been normalised.
Softmax
Turns a list of scores into shares that are all positive and sum to 1. Raise e to each score, divide by the total. Bigger scores get disproportionately bigger shares; only differences between scores matter.A dial between "pick the single best" and "average everyone," set by how spread out the scores are.
Attention weight
One share from a softmax row: the fraction of this query's attention that goes to that key. The weights mix the values.The heat in every heatmap on this page.
Head
One complete attention lookup with its own small WQ, WK, WV, so it can learn one kind of question. A layer runs many heads in parallel on slices of the vector and concatenates their outputs through WO.A newsroom: each reporter reads the same document for one thing (names, dates, who said what) and the editor merges the notes.
Layer, residual stream
One layer = one attention block followed by one MLP, each adding its output back onto the token's vector. That running vector, carried through all the layers, is the residual stream. Modern models stack 30 to 120 layers.A shared whiteboard per token. Every block reads it and writes a little more onto it; nothing is erased.
MLP
The other half of every layer: a small two-matrix network applied to each token on its own. It stores facts and transforms features; it cannot look at other positions. Attention moves information between tokens; the MLP thinks about what has arrived.Attention is the postal service, the MLP is what each house does with the mail.
Weights, parameters W
The learned matrices (WQ, WK, WV, WO, the MLP matrices, the embedding table). A "70B model" has seventy billion of these numbers. They are fixed after training; only the context changes at inference.The model's long-term memory. Context is its short-term memory.
Loss, gradient descent
Loss is a number saying how wrong the next-token prediction was. The gradient says which direction to nudge every weight to make it less wrong. Training repeats "predict, measure, nudge" trillions of times.Walking downhill in fog: you cannot see the valley but you can feel the slope under your feet.
Causal mask −∞
Before softmax, set every score for a future token to −∞ so its weight becomes exactly 0. Position i can only read positions ≤ i. Required for next-token prediction and the reason keys and values can be cached.Reading with a card covering the rest of the page.
Positional encoding, RoPE θ
Attention has no built-in sense of order, so position is injected. RoPE rotates each query and key by an angle proportional to its position, so the dot product depends only on how far apart two tokens are.Clock hands: compare two times by the angle between the hands, not by where the clock is on the wall.
KV cache
During generation, every layer's keys and values for all past tokens are stored so a new token only computes its own q, k, v and attends over the stored ones. Grows linearly with context; at long contexts it is what fills the GPU.Notes kept from earlier pages so you never re-read the book to write the next sentence.
Context window n
The number of tokens the model can attend over at once: your prompt, the files, the tool results, and everything it has generated so far in the reply. Scores are an n × n matrix per head per layer.The size of the desk. The n² bill is the cost of having every paper able to see every other paper.
Logits
The final scores over the vocabulary for the next token, one number per possible token, before a softmax turns them into probabilities. Sampling picks from that distribution.Same softmax trick, used one last time to choose the next tile.
FLOPs, HBM, SRAM
FLOPs: floating-point operations, the compute bill. HBM: the GPU's large, slow main memory (80 GB on an H100). SRAM: the tiny, fast memory beside each compute unit (about 228 KB). Modern attention is limited by moving bytes between them, not by arithmetic.A chef with a huge pantry down the hall and a tiny counter. Speed is about how often you walk to the pantry.
Decoder-only Transformer
The architecture of GPT, Llama, Claude: a stack of causal-attention + MLP layers that predicts the next token, repeated one token at a time. "Decoder" is historical; there is no separate encoder.One machine, one job: given everything so far, what comes next.

01 · THE BOTTLENECK

Before attention, distance was destiny

Recurrent networks read a sentence one token at a time and squeeze everything seen so far into one fixed-size state. Each step is a hop. To use the word "company" when predicting "public", the signal has to survive every hop in between, and each hop blurs it a little.

Attention replaces the chain with a direct wire. Any position can read any earlier position in a single step, so the path length between two tokens is always one.

Hover a word. The arcs trace the path back to the word it depends on; the readout shows how much signal survives.

02 · SOFT LOOKUP

A dictionary you can differentiate

A hash map answers a query by finding the one key that matches exactly. Attention answers by scoring the query against every key, turning the scores into a probability distribution with softmax, and returning the weighted average of the values. Nothing is discontinuous, so gradients flow and the keys, queries and values can all be learned.

The three names are the whole idea (glossary). Query: what I am looking for. Key: what each entry advertises. Value: what each entry hands back if chosen.

low β → averages everyone · high β → hard lookup

Drag the query (the ring). Score = β·(q·k); weights = softmax(scores); output = Σ weight × value. The big swatch is the blended output colour.

Softmax, by hand

Two operations. Exponentiate every score, then divide each by the sum. Exponentiation makes everything positive and turns score differences into weight ratios: a key scoring 1 higher gets e ≈ 2.7× the weight, wherever the absolute level sits. Dividing by the sum makes the row add to 1, so the output is a true average of the values. Subtracting the row maximum first changes nothing (it cancels in the division) and keeps exp from overflowing; a masked score of −∞ becomes exp(−∞) = 0 and drops out.

Read down a column to follow one key through the pipeline; read across the last row to see the distribution. Push β up and watch the row become one-hot.

03 · WHERE Q, K, V COME FROM

Nobody tells "sat" what to look for

The query is not chosen, it is computed: q = x·WQ, where x is the token's current vector (its embedding plus whatever earlier layers wrote into that position) and WQ is one learned matrix shared by every token. Keys and values are the same story with WK and WV. A head therefore learns a kind of question, and each token asks that question about itself.

The matrices start random. Attention starts uniform. Then gradient descent on next-token prediction nudges WQ and WK, a little per example, in whatever direction made the prediction better. Across millions of sentences, verbs' queries drift toward nouns' keys because knowing the subject helps predict what comes after a verb. Neither matrix knows what a subject is. The score is really one bilinear form xi WQ WKT xjT, factored in two so a key can be cached before its future query exists.

Watch it happen below. Four fixed token vectors, two random 4×2 projections, and a training signal that rewards "attend to the token that precedes you in meaning" (the stand-in for "whatever predicts the next token").

Left: softmax(QKᵀ/√d) as it trains; outlined cells are the targets. Right: loss per step and the two projection matrices being nudged. The gradient reaching WQ is (weights − target) × K, pushed back through the token vectors: every wrong weight pulls the query toward the key it should have matched.

04 · THE EQUATION

Four tokens, four numbers each, every step visible

Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax(QKT / √dk) V

Each token's embedding is projected three ways by learned matrices to get its query, key and value. Then: multiply queries by keys to get scores, divide by the square root of the key width, softmax each row, and use each row as the mixing weights over values. Step through it.

Why the √dk?

A dot product of two random vectors with unit-variance entries has variance equal to their width. At width 512 the raw scores wander ±22, softmax turns into a one-hot argmax, and the gradient through it goes to zero. Dividing by √d pins the variance to one regardless of width. 05 · MASKS AND HEADS

One head is a pattern. A model is a chorus of them.

A decoder predicts the next token, so position i must not see positions after it. The causal mask sets those scores to −∞ before softmax, which makes the attention matrix lower-triangular. It is also what makes the KV cache possible later: a token's keys and values never change once computed.

One softmax can only express one weighting per query. Multi-head attention runs several smaller attentions in parallel on different projections and concatenates the results, so one head can track the previous token while another hunts for repeated names. 06 · POSITION

Attention is a set operation. Position has to be injected.

Nothing in QKT knows where a token sits; shuffle the inputs and the outputs shuffle identically. The original Transformer added sinusoidal position vectors to the embeddings. Modern models use rotary position embeddings (RoPE): split each query and key into 2-D pairs and rotate pair i by angle m·θi, where m is the position and θi shrinks geometrically across pairs.

Rotation is the trick. The dot product of two rotated vectors depends only on the difference of their angles, so q·k depends on m − n, the relative offset, and shifting the whole sequence changes nothing. High-frequency pairs resolve neighbours; low-frequency pairs resolve paragraphs.

Four frequency bands of a d=8 head, θi = 10000−2i/d. The dot product never moves when you drag Δ, because only m − n matters.

07 · INDUCTION HEADS

The circuit that lets me learn from your examples

Stack two layers and something new becomes possible. A previous-token head in layer one writes "the token before me was X" into each position. An induction head in layer two then asks: where earlier in the context did the current token appear, and what came right after it? It attends there and copies that successor forward.

That is a general-purpose pattern completer that was never trained on your specific pattern. It is the mechanism behind in-context learning: show me a format once and I continue it; paste a name and I spell it the same way on page forty. Induction heads appear abruptly during training and coincide with the model's sudden ability to use long context.

08 · THE n² BILL

Every token looks at every token. That is the cost and the point.

Scores are an n×n matrix per head per layer, so compute and memory for the scores grow quadratically with context length. At generation time we avoid recomputing the past by caching every layer's keys and values, the KV cache. It grows linearly with context and, for long contexts, it is the thing that fills the GPU and the thing every decode step has to stream through.

That is why the frontier of attention research is mostly a frontier of KV-cache engineering: fewer key/value heads (MQA, GQA), compressed latents (MLA), windows and sparsity, and kernels that never write the big matrix at all.

Lines: KV cache for this model's depth and head size under four KV layouts. The vertical marker is your context. Attention-share estimates FLOPs of the two score matmuls against the linear layers, prefill, no MoE accounting.

Prefill, decode, and where attention sits in a layer

Two phases, same equation, opposite bottlenecks. Prefill reads your whole prompt at once: every query against every key, one big matmul, compute-bound, and this is where the n² score matrix and FlashAttention's tiling matter. Decode then produces one token at a time: a single new query against every cached key, so each step is tiny arithmetic but must stream the entire KV cache from memory. Decode is bandwidth-bound, which is why GQA, MLA, sparse selection and paging target the cache rather than the FLOPs.

xnormattention+ xnormMLP+ xx′

One layer, end to end: normalise the token's vector, run attention, add the result back onto the vector; normalise again, run the MLP, add that back. The vector carried through the additions is the residual stream. Attention is the only step that reads other positions. Every layer repeats this, and the final vector is projected to logits.

09 · FLASHATTENTION

The matrix that is never written down

A GPU does matmuls far faster than it can move bytes between its big slow memory (HBM) and the tiny fast memory next to each compute unit (SRAM). Naive attention computes the full score matrix, writes it to HBM, reads it back for softmax, writes probabilities, reads them again to multiply by V. For long contexts the matrix does not even fit.

FlashAttention is IO-aware: it loads one block of queries and streams blocks of keys and values through SRAM, keeping a running maximum and running sum so softmax can be computed incrementally (online softmax) without ever seeing a full row. The output is exact. Memory for scores drops from n² to the size of one tile, and wall-clock drops because HBM traffic drops. FlashAttention-2 reorganised the work across threads; FlashAttention-3 overlaps matmul with softmax on Hopper and runs in FP8.

10 · THE FRONTIER

Ten years of bending one equation

Each advance below keeps softmax(QKT)V as the primitive and attacks one term of the bill: the memory of the cache, the bandwidth to read it, the quadratic score matrix, or the kernel's traffic.

Where 2026 stands

No 2026 result has displaced MLA, FlashAttention or the sparse indexer. What changed is that the 2025 ideas reached flagship scale: DeepSeek-V3.2 shipped DSA in production, every major open hybrid (Qwen3-Next, Kimi Linear, Nemotron 3) settled near a 3:1 linear-to-full ratio, and kernels were rewritten for Blackwell. The live arguments are (1) whether linear hybrids survive agentic workloads where exact recall matters, with MiniMax's reversal to full attention as the counter-signal, (2) whether learned sparsity or sliding windows win at 1M+ tokens, and (3) how much of a model's work can be moved out of attention into cheaper lookups and rewired residuals. Not stalled; the frontier moved from the equation to its neighbours.

11 · WHY IT MADE ME

What this has to do with the thing you are talking to

I am a decoder-only Transformer. Each token I produce is the output of a stack of layers, and in every layer the only operation that mixes information across positions is attention. The MLPs think about one position at a time; attention is how your instructions, the files you opened, the tool results and my own earlier words reach the position that is deciding the next token.

Three properties of this one operation account for most of what I can do:

Content-addressable memory. I do not retrieve by position but by match. A query shaped like "the function I was asked to edit" lands on that function wherever it sits in a 200k-token session.

Learning without weight updates. Induction-style circuits turn my context into a working memory I can pattern-match against. Few-shot prompting, following a style, or using a tool whose schema I saw once are all attention reading the examples back.

A cost that could be engineered down. Without the causal KV cache, grouped heads, latent compression and IO-aware kernels, a long conversation would be unaffordable at every step. Everything in chapter ten is what makes a long session cheap enough to exist.

The chapters above are deliberately simplified: real heads are noisy, superposed and shared across many jobs, and attention is interleaved with MLPs, normalisation and residual connections that carry the actual information between layers.
